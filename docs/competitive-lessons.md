# Competitive Lessons

Quick market research suggests adjacent ghost and camera horror apps can still earn meaningful downloads despite weak execution:

- Spirit Whisper - Ghost talker: roughly 72k downloads, 11k recent monthly downloads, 4.03 rating.
- Ghost Detector & Camera Radar: roughly 110k downloads, 1.2k recent monthly downloads, 2.69 rating.
- Ghost Hunter: Exorcist Horror: roughly 2.6M downloads, 10k recent monthly downloads, 3.24 rating.

## Observed Weaknesses

- Cheap-looking UI and store art.
- Generic horror visuals.
- Too many ads.
- Poor trust.
- Vague "ghost detector" gimmicks.
- Laggy or glitchy gameplay.
- Users complain it feels fake or not like an actual game.
- Users want more ghost types, items, evidence, and replayability.

## Design Implications For The Watcher

- Do not make it feel like a fake ghost detector.
- Do not show ads before the first real scare.
- The first 30 seconds must prove the app reacts to the player for real.
- Prioritize polish, camera feel, audio, and tension.
- Use local face and camera tracking as the differentiator.
- Make the caught card and caught clip central, not an afterthought.
- Add evidence language: caught frame, failure reason, timestamp, entity name.
- Make the content pack-based: first free entity, paid future entities.
- Keep trust high: camera frames are processed locally unless the user shares.
- Avoid requiring true AR or dual camera in v1. Use pseudo-AR and camera overlays first.
